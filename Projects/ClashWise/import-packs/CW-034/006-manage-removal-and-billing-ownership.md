## Remove access cleanly

@action: As Admin, open the prepared obsolete synthetic member's removal confirmation, then cancel.

Removing an organisation member frees their seat and ends organisation access. Before confirming, inspect any projects, integrations, or responsibilities that depend on that person and transfer ownership where needed.

For project-only removal, use the project roster instead. That narrower action ends access to one workspace without removing the person's organisation membership.

## Keep billing details text-first

@action: Point to Upgrade plan and Manage billing without opening a live payment surface.

The organisation owns its subscription. Admins can adjust the plan and seats or open the billing portal. Prices, tax, minimums, cancellation mechanics, and allowance figures are deliberately left in the live billing and help pages, not frozen into this video.

Never expose real billing details during training capture.

## Diagnose the common access failures

No seats available means active members and pending invitations already consume the purchased capacity. Review both states before buying more or removing somebody.

An invitation that stays Pending usually means the recipient has not signed in with the exact invited address. A member who can sign in but cannot see Riverside may be in the organisation without project membership. A person who sees Riverside but cannot manage its roster is probably a Project Member rather than Owner.

A greyed organisation control indicates the current user is a Member or the organisation is read-only. Switch roles or restore entitlement through the proper Admin workflow instead of repeatedly clicking the disabled control.

## Transfer responsibility before removal

Before removing an Admin, confirm another Admin remains. Before removing a Project Owner, confirm the project retains another Owner. Review matrices, clash sets, scheduled sessions, connected applications, and external responsibility assignments associated with the person.

Removal changes access, not historical authorship. Existing audit events and valid coordination records must continue to show who performed the earlier work.

Finish by verifying the synthetic coordinator has one occupied organisation seat, ordinary Member rights, and only the Riverside project visibility required for the next lesson.
