## Monitor coverage rather than activity theatre

@screen: Show total coverage, per-lane progress, unassigned count, and prepared rate or projection.

Coverage counts clashes once even when one issue belongs to more than one lane. In no lane exposes work the chosen axis failed to cover.

Rate and projection appear only after enough real progress exists to estimate them. Do not promise a completion time before the board displays one.

## Understand drift

@screen: Show a prepared lane with reassigned-in or moved-out drift.

Drift reports that work moved after the split. It is context, not an error.

If a participant approaches work owned by another lane, they can still inspect it as a member, but should coordinate ownership before changing it. Use reassignment or a flag when responsibility needs to move.

## Split by an existing axis

Parallel lanes are derived from a real property or coordination axis already present in the set, such as responsible contact, level, discipline, or another offered grouping. They are not arbitrary chat rooms with hand-typed names.

Choose an axis that creates meaningful ownership and enough work per lane. A split into many one-clash lanes adds navigation without useful parallelism. A single oversized Unknown lane exposes missing data that should be corrected before the meeting.

Review the lane counts and unassigned coverage before inviting people to work.

## Understand what a lane does and does not fence

Lane ownership tells the room who is leading that cohort. A signed-in member can still inspect the combined session according to their set access; the lane view is a working filter, not a new security boundary.

During the split, track reviewed counts, decisions, actions, flags, and drift. Reassign work when the wrong lane owns it. Flag a clash when it needs central discussion rather than making a unilateral cross-lane decision.

When lanes regroup, reconcile the flagged and drifted work first. The combined activity record should explain how the parallel conversations became one meeting outcome.
