## Group to reveal the work packages

@action: Point out the standalone Clash # column and Coordination column group, then group the filtered result by Level and Priority and sort by Due Date.

Clash number stays in its own column because it is permanent identity, not a grouping label. Review,
status, priority, contact, and related work fields sit together under the Coordination header so
the grid separates identity from the decisions made about it.

Grouping arranges matching rows into visible packages. Select Level first and Priority second. The ordinal on each field shows the grouping order, and every group header reports its clash count.

Sorting changes the order within the result. Sort by Due Date so the most urgent work is easy to scan. Grouping and sorting do not change any clash data; they change how the worklist is presented.

This distinction matters when explaining the screen in a session. A group is a view of a cohort, not automatically a Navisworks clash group.

## Mark review progress separately from decisions

@action: Open two prepared rows, mark them reviewed through the available review control, and return to the All, Reviewed, and Not reviewed quick filter.

Mark two clashes reviewed after actually inspecting them. Review is available during ordinary
day-to-day triage as well as inside a live session. Reviewed means the team has looked at the issue.
It does not automatically change status, priority, ownership, or closure.

Closure is also separate from a matrix workflow status. Use the dedicated Open or Closed lifecycle control to close and reopen a clash. The reserved status name Closed is no longer available as a matrix option, because a recommendation must never silently close the underlying clash.

Use the review quick filter to prove the two rows moved from Not reviewed into Reviewed. During an active coordination session, review actions can also be attributed to that session and included in its record.

Keeping review progress separate from decisions prevents a dangerous shortcut: a clash can be reviewed and still require action.
