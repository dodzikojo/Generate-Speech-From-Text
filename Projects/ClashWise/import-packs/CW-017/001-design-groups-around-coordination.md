## Compare the flat list with useful groups

@visual: Show the twenty ungrouped Riverside clashes, then the finished Level, grid band, and system hierarchy in Clash Detective.

Grouping does not change clash geometry or detect anything new. It reorganises existing results into native Clash Detective groups.

The finished structure should help someone own or review a package. Level, grid zone, and system are useful because they answer where the work is and what kind of system it belongs to.

Avoid a hierarchy that is technically precise but produces one group per clash. The goal is fewer, meaningful packages.

## Decide how much structure is enough

@screen: Show the prepared data distribution for levels, grids, and systems with expected bucket sizes.

Before adding rules, inspect the source values. The training test has several levels, grid zones, and systems, plus a few values that are blank or uncommon.

One or two rules are usually enough. Every extra rule multiplies combinations and can leave many buckets below the minimum size.

Choose a minimum size of two for this demonstration so single-clash buckets remain ungrouped and visible for review.
